# Isolated success does not establish transfer

## Key takeaway

Performance under a simplified drill’s conditions does not by itself establish performance under whole-stroke conditions.

## Summary

Removing the breath, a limb action or an unsupported balance demand changes the task. Some simplification can help observation, but it can also hide the interaction causing the difficulty. Use it to frame technique questions; it does not prescribe one exact movement or establish an individual diagnosis.

## Worked use and boundary

A board-supported kick removes some arm and balance demands. The full stroke adds them back. The reason to return to a short familiar whole task is to test that transition, not to satisfy a fixed drill quota.

## Statement
Performance under a simplified drill’s conditions does not by itself establish performance under whole-stroke conditions.

## Explanation
Removing the breath, a limb action or an unsupported balance demand changes the task. Some simplification can help observation, but it can also hide the interaction causing the difficulty.

## Evidence and source synthesis
This is an explicit logical limit: a result measured under altered support, timing or breathing conditions is not itself a result measured in the target whole stroke. It does not claim that part-practice is better or worse than whole-practice. The Cochrane directness discussion is an analogy about applicability, not evidence of a swimming training effect. The actual support for retaining a particular drill must include its observed target-task contribution.

## Derivation and applications
A drill followed by a short whole-stroke retest checks the target task. An already integrated action may need no isolation at all.

## Scope
Supported exercises, slow rehearsal, equipment-aided practice and return to ordinary surface swimming.

## Limits and alternatives
Whole-stroke failure does not prove the drill was useless. Readiness and safe support remain prerequisites; removing support is not automatic progression.

## Qualification and challenge
Only actual target-task results address transfer. When target demands are unchanged, calling a test “isolated” may be misleading.
