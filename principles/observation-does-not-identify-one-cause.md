# Observation does not identify one cause

## Key takeaway

A sensation, visible motion or single performance change can be consistent with more than one explanation.

## Summary

Video can show when the head turns, but does not directly measure drag, ventilation or tissue pathology. A successful repeat can also reflect rest, pace, attention or a different push-off. Use it to frame technique questions; it does not prescribe one exact movement or establish an individual diagnosis.

## Worked use and boundary

“I sink after breathing” can describe perceived slowing, actual body pitch or loss of air control. A side observation of the event can distinguish some options; sensation alone cannot diagnose the cause.

The general self-coaching conclusion is an inference from measurement limits.

## Derivation and applications
Observation-first notes, an alternative hypothesis, a safe comparison and targeted external feedback are different ways to reduce ambiguity.

## Scope
Personal symptom interpretation and low-risk technique comparisons.

## Limits and alternatives
A local preference can be useful without establishing its mechanism. Do not perform deliberately unsafe controls or claim a small personal comparison is a randomized experiment.

## Qualification and challenge
Independent, suitable measurements or convergent repeated observations can strengthen a particular explanation.
