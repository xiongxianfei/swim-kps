# Buoyancy and orientation are distinct

## Key takeaway

A body can have enough buoyant support to float while adopting an orientation that is not horizontal.

## Summary

Net vertical support and rotational balance are different questions. Body shape, air volume, mass distribution and movement affect the practical floating configuration. Use it to frame technique questions; it does not prescribe one exact movement or establish an individual diagnosis.

## Worked use and boundary

Legs that sit lower during a supported float are not proof of inadequate buoyancy. The distribution of support and weight, body shape and movement affect orientation separately.

## Derivation and applications
A leg-sinking sensation is not sufficient evidence of a fault that requires rigid bracing. A supported balance check and a stroke-timing check investigate different possibilities.

## Scope
Low-demand support and body-position reasoning in suitable pool conditions.

## Limits and alternatives
Safe air access is not guaranteed by theoretical buoyancy. No instruction to hold breath, become rigid or test floating alone follows.

## Qualification and challenge
If visible alignment changes are attributable to dynamic action rather than floating balance, this explanation may be irrelevant to the observed issue.

[University Physics Volume 1](../references/moebs-2016-university-physics-volume-1.md#moment-arms-and-hydrostatic-buoyancy) contributes: A force’s rotational effect depends on its direction and moment arm; hydrostatic buoyancy equals the weight of displaced fluid.

[Float to Live](../references/rnli-undated-float-to-live.md#floating-and-individual-body-position) contributes: Floating can involve gentle movement and varies between people; perfectly horizontal legs are not a universal condition.
