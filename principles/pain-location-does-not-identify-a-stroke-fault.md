# Pain location does not identify a stroke fault

## Key takeaway

A pain report at a joint does not uniquely identify the movement error or tissue condition responsible.

## Summary

Technique, load, past conditions and non-swimming causes can be relevant. A plausible mechanical story does not replace assessment. Use it to frame technique questions; it does not prescribe one exact movement or establish an individual diagnosis.

## Worked use and boundary

Pain near a knee does not identify foot turnout, a ligament injury or a particular muscle problem. Stop the aggravating task and use appropriate assessment rather than trying progressively larger corrections.

These are NHS guidance, not trials of particular corrections.

## Derivation and applications
Stop the aggravating experiment and obtain appropriate feedback or healthcare assessment. That boundary is a safety decision supported by the uncertainty, not a diagnosis.

## Scope
Pain arising during or around swimming and related movement.

## Limits and alternatives
This package does not prescribe rehabilitation, forced mobility, exercise through pain or a return-to-swim plan.

## Qualification and challenge
A clinician’s assessed cause may narrow the possibilities for that individual; the package should not extrapolate it to all swimmers.
