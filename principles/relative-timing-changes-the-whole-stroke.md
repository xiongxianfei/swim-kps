# Relative timing changes the whole stroke

## Key takeaway

The effects of component movements depend partly on their timing relative to other movements and the breathing opportunity.

## Summary

The same arm and leg shapes arranged differently can alter interruption, air access and forward continuity. Isolated correctness therefore does not specify whole-stroke timing. Use it to frame technique questions; it does not prescribe one exact movement or establish an individual diagnosis.

## Worked use and boundary

The same recognizable arm and leg actions can be arranged differently in time. Check the kick relative to returning arms and breathing, rather than assume the parts’ isolated success determines their combination.

## Derivation and applications
Glide and reintegration comparisons investigate timing instead of enforcing four pauses or one universal overlap. Selecting comfortable continuity is an authored self-coaching goal.

## Scope
Breaststroke phase relationships and, qualitatively, coordination questions in crawl.

## Limits and alternatives
The study does not identify the best novice pattern. A convenient verbal sequence is not a precise phase boundary.

## Qualification and challenge
If the supposed timing adjustment was not executed, or the effect disappears at comparable effort, the individual conclusion remains unresolved.
