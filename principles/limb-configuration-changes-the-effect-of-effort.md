# Limb configuration changes the effect of effort

## Key takeaway

Changing limb orientation and motion can change the direction and useful contribution of the interaction with water.

## Summary

Increasing effort before a workable interaction is established need not increase useful forward motion proportionally. Preparation and effort can therefore have different functions. Use it to frame technique questions; it does not prescribe one exact movement or establish an individual diagnosis.

## Worked use and boundary

Two comfortable foot setups may produce different sensations or motion under similar effort. That makes preparation a useful question; it does not establish a universal foot angle or justify forcing the joints.

## Derivation and applications
A controlled foot-preparation check, a comfortable arm-preparation check or a brief slow rehearsal are possible applications. No one-second pause, rigid joint angle or maximal force is entailed.

## Scope
Low-demand technique exploration with normal comfortable movement range.

## Limits and alternatives
Visible preparation is not a measurement of fluid force, metabolic cost or joint load. Direction and timing vary through an articulated motion. A coach’s preferred geometry cannot justify forced knee width, maximum turnout or a locked elbow. A source’s muscle explanation is not an individual diagnosis. Less usable propulsion can have causes other than orientation, including timing, effort and an unsuitable comparison.

## Qualification and challenge
If the visible change is achieved but whole-stroke control or propulsion does not improve, the chosen adjustment may not solve the limiting problem.
