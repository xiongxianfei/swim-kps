# Recovery repositions limbs and alters resistance

## Key takeaway

A recovery movement changes both the readiness of the limb for the next action and the swimmer’s interaction with water.

## Summary

A longer or wider return can change time spent in a configuration as well as its resistance. A useful pull is therefore not evaluated independently of what must follow it. Use it to frame technique questions; it does not prescribe one exact movement or establish an individual diagnosis.

## Worked use and boundary

In breaststroke, bringing the hands forward prepares the next cycle while moving through water. A shorter or faster return may change both resistance and coordination, so its value belongs to the whole task.

## Derivation and applications
A compact breaststroke recovery is a defensible starting reference. It remains one response, not an imperative to minimize every motion or recover as fast as possible.

## Scope
Surface breaststroke and questions about returning limbs to an effective next phase.

## Limits and alternatives
This relationship does not imply that the fastest or narrowest possible recovery is optimal. A trajectory change can alter comfort, breathing, duration and later propulsion together. A stage may choose a direct comfortable recovery as a reference without declaring that a visually compact movement is lower-drag or that faster limb motion always reduces total cost.

## Qualification and challenge
If a more compact or quicker recovery increases discomfort or disrupts breathing/timing, it is not a successful application.
