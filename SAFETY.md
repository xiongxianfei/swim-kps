# Safety for self directed pool learning

## Key takeaway

Self-coaching does not mean swimming alone or testing unsafe conditions.

## Summary

This boundary applies before every in-water task: dependable air access and support, suitable supervision, no breath restriction, and stopping rather than diagnosing pain through repeated experiments.

## Non negotiable starting conditions
Self-coaching means I choose the question; it does **not** mean I swim alone. Use a suitable lifeguarded pool with a companion. A weak or inexperienced swimmer needs appropriate close in-person assistance; a general lifeguard presence does not replace that assistance. A kickboard is a training aid, not protection against drowning.

For face-in or supported tasks, choose an allowed area with dependable footing, a secure handhold and a known way to clear the mouth immediately. If I cannot already stop and regain air/support, I need an instructor to establish that skill before attempting an unsupported drill. Reading the stage is not evidence that I can execute it safely. I can rehearse the movement on land meanwhile.

Keep ordinary breathing and rest. Do not hyperventilate, suppress breaths, chase underwater distance or force a longer glide. Stop for pain, dizziness, unusual breathlessness, panic, disorientation, loss of support or repeated water inhalation. Regain support and get help; do not repeat a harmful condition as a control. Severe breathing difficulty or sudden severe symptoms require urgent help. Persistent or worsening pain, swelling or difficulty with ordinary movement needs appropriate healthcare assessment, not a guessed stroke correction.

No diving, racing pullouts, flip turns, open-water transfer or rehabilitation is taught here. Film only with pool permission and consent; an observer who is filming must not replace someone providing essential close safety assistance.

## Support is part of the task
A secure handhold with reliable footing permits a brief face-in or supported movement task only when I can already return to air. To end a supported prone task, keep the hold, use the recovery previously learned, bring the feet under toward the floor and regain a stable upright position before releasing support. If this sequence is not already reliable, an instructor must establish it in person; this sentence is not a substitute for that teaching.

A jellyfish float, new back float or unsupported glide is not required to unlock the rest of this library. RNLI’s back-floating guidance addresses air access and water safety; it does not prove a particular prone breaststroke posture. Natural leg sinking alone is not an instruction to panic or thrash, but inability to regain air is always a stopping condition.

## What the records actually support
- [Red Cross swimming safety](references/american-red-cross-undated-swimming-safety.md): lifeguarded areas, a buddy and appropriate close assistance for inexperienced swimmers.
- [CDC investigation of dangerous breath-holding](references/boyd-2015-dangerous-underwater-breath-holding.md): no safety threshold for hyperventilation/underwater endurance is derived here.
- [RNLI Float to Live](references/rnli-undated-float-to-live.md): emergency/supportive floating, not a prone-stroke lesson.
- [NHS knee symptoms](references/nhs-undated-knee-pain.md) and [NHS shoulder symptoms](references/nhs-undated-shoulder-pain.md): symptoms may need healthcare assessment; a cue is not a diagnosis.

Use local emergency assistance when serious symptoms arise; ordinary self-coaching must not delay urgent help. This document does not diagnose a condition, prescribe rehabilitation or grant medical clearance.

## Local stop rule

Self-coaching does not mean swimming alone. Use a suitable lifeguarded pool with a buddy and the close assistance needed; weak or inexperienced swimmers need an appropriately capable helper within reach. Attempt only tasks in which breathing, stopping and regaining support are already dependable; otherwise use land study or in-person instruction first. Do not hyperventilate, restrict breaths, prolong submersion or force painful joint positions. Stop for pain, dizziness, distress or unreliable air/support; get immediate assistance for serious symptoms.
