# Self coaching learning and feedback model

## Key takeaway

Explain how observations inform a working choice, and distinguish current performance from retention and transfer.

## Summary

A successful safe attempt can justify keeping a cue provisionally. It does not establish lasting learning or a universal mechanism. Separate intended action, observed action, outcome and interpretation; retention and transfer concern later or changed conditions.

## Local explanatory basis

A sensation, visible motion or single performance change can be consistent with more than one explanation. Performance under a simplified drill’s conditions does not by itself establish performance under whole-stroke conditions.

## Purpose
Explain how observations inform a working choice, and distinguish current performance from retention and transfer.

## Representation
```text
safe question → observation → candidate explanations
     → one safe comparison → documented result
     → keep provisionally / modify / discard / unresolved / seek help
```

Separate four records: **what I intended**, **what I actually did**, **what happened**, and **what I infer**. A useful choice can remain provisional even when its mechanism is uncertain.

## Boundary and essential terms

The represented system is the learner's use of observations and feedback to update a working action, followed by evidence of whether it remains usable. A **cue** is a brief instruction or image directing attention. **Feedback** is information about the action or result, from the swimmer's perception or an outside observer. **Performance** is what happens on the current attempt. **Retention** concerns later use of the learned skill; **transfer** concerns its use under changed conditions. Writing a choice in a record is not the same as retaining the motor skill.

Feedback can reveal a mismatch between intended and observed action, helping the learner select a different action or revise an explanation. This is an information-and-decision account, not a claim to model the neural mechanism of skill acquisition. A result may improve because of immediate guidance or familiar conditions without establishing lasting learning.

## Retention and transfer

```text
  intended action → observed action and result → feedback → working choice
                                                      ↓
                      later performance in comparable conditions: retention question
                      performance in a changed task: transfer question
```

The arrows describe information flow. Later observations should state assistance, cue availability, task and conditions. A result with changed conditions cannot isolate forgetting from a new task demand. The Model does not prescribe removing essential safety assistance to test independence.

The [learning versus performance review](../references/soderstrom-2015-learning-versus-performance.md) supports the distinction between current performance and lasting learning at the inspected abstract's scope. It does not validate this decision loop, a particular cue or a feedback schedule.

## Worked example

**Constructed case:** A learner follows an arm-return cue successfully while prompted. On a later occasion, the same event occurs under comparable safe conditions without that verbal prompt. This is evidence relevant to retention, not proof of a universal mechanism. If the event disappears only when a breathing turn is added, the changed task raises a transfer/coordination question. It does not prove that the original skill was never learned. No outcome here describes an actual learner.

## Assumptions
The person can safely complete and stop the selected task with appropriate supervision/support. Tests are small, voluntary and never deliberately painful or breath-restricting.

## Implications
Use an externally informed movement reference when the action is unknown. Form a technique hypothesis only when there is something to observe. A drill is selected for a question, not completed because it is next in a syllabus.

## Evidence and evaluation
[Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions, version 6.5](../references/cochrane-2024-handbook-v6-5.md#indirectness-relative-to-the-target-question) provides a general applicability caution, not a validation of this personal protocol. [Swimming Safety](../references/american-red-cross-undated-swimming-safety.md#supervision-companions-and-close-assistance) defines safety guidance used in the entry gate. The loop itself is authored design.

## Limits
Order, fatigue, practice, expectation, push-off and pace are confounders. A few repeats cannot establish causality or justify treatment of pain.

## Stages and operational summaries
The stroke Practice contains capability stages within one file. A stage combines a local goal, contextual explanation, selected procedure, observable signal, stopping conditions and a next/fallback decision. Stages can be revisited; their order is a route, not a universal curriculum.

An opening Summary routes the reader. A stage Summary compresses what is needed for that decision. The Current Practice Summary records actual personal state once, separately from reference technique. Externally linked objects supply provenance and deeper variants; they must not carry information essential to the immediate safe action.

This is a documentation/decision design, not an empirical claim that staged files improve motor learning. Each stage lists its canonical dependencies so an upstream change can trigger an inline-summary review.

## Local evidence summary


[Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions, version 6.5](../references/cochrane-2024-handbook-v6-5.md#indirectness-relative-to-the-target-question) contributes: Evidence may be indirect when study populations, interventions, comparisons or outcomes differ from the target question.

[Swimming Safety](../references/american-red-cross-undated-swimming-safety.md#supervision-companions-and-close-assistance) contributes: Guidance calls for lifeguarded areas, a swimming companion, and close assistance for weak or inexperienced swimmers.
