# Effect of The Swimmers Head Position on Passive Drag

## Key takeaway

A controlled passive-towing study tested head/body configurations in experienced male swimmers.

## Summary

A controlled passive-towing study tested head/body configurations in experienced male swimmers. It supports configuration-sensitive passive drag in that setting. It does not establish an ideal beginner breathing posture or diagnose a perceived sinking sensation.


## Current inspection and use limit

The PubMed response did not expose useful article content, and the PMC host presented a browser challenge. The existing extraction is retained, not newly verified in full text. Its passive-towing and experienced-swimmer scope cannot establish an optimal active beginner breathing posture.

## Head position under passive towing

Head position affected passive underwater drag in experienced male swimmers under the study’s towing conditions.

**Locator:** Indexed abstract; ten male swimmers, passive towing

## Participants and transfer limits

The participants were ten male swimmers with substantial competition experience, measured during passive underwater towing. They were not adult beginners performing an active breathing stroke.

**Locator:** Materials and methods: participants and experimental procedure.

## What this source does not establish

Not an active beginner-breathing trial; not proof of a universal gaze angle or that a personal cue lowers drag.
