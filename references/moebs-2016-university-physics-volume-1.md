The textbook supplies classical net-force reasoning and the hydrostatic displaced-water account of buoyancy. These explain constraints on motion and support. A stroke model needs additional geometry, flow, body movement and empirical information.

## Net force and translational motion

In the constant-mass classical approximation, net external force determines centre-of-mass acceleration.

**Locator:** Section 5.3

## Moment arms and hydrostatic buoyancy

A force’s rotational effect depends on its direction and moment arm; hydrostatic buoyancy equals the weight of displaced fluid.

**Locator:** [Section 10.6 — Torque](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/10-6-torque) and [Section 14.4 — Buoyancy](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/14-4-archimedes-principle-and-buoyancy)
