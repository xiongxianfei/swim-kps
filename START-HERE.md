# Start here one stage one question

## Key takeaway

Choose one safe question and one stage, not the whole library.

## Summary

Swim supports adult self-coaching of breaststroke and front crawl. Start with prerequisites, use an unknown movement’s reference or a known problem’s troubleshooting, and finish with one actual observation and next decision.

## Choose a safe task first
Use a suitable lifeguarded pool with a companion; an inexperienced swimmer needs appropriate close assistance. The task needs a dependable way to stop, clear the mouth and regain support. Unknown recovery, pain or repeated failed air access routes to appropriate in-person help—not more force or a longer attempt. Knowledge study remains possible on land. Both Practices repeat the complete safety boundary.

## Read at the right depth
The stage Summary tells me the goal, reason, action, observation, success signal and next step. The procedure supplies the actual setup and sequence. Read both before a new task. Deeper links explain sources and alternatives; opening them is not required to understand the stage.

Choose a finishable familiar segment, not a required full length. Test one comfortable adjustment with ordinary rest and breathing; confirm execution before judging the outcome. A supported exercise is not evidence of free-swimming propulsion. Reintegrate only when that task is safe.

## Finish with an honest decision
Keep provisionally, modify, discard, unresolved or get help. Record sensation as sensation, visible movement as observation and explanation as a hypothesis. Update only the actual stroke’s Current Practice Summary. Do not populate a new achievement from a recommendation in this library.
