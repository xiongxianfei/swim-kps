# Use Swim KPS for one real question

## Key takeaway

Choose one stroke, one safe question and one observation; do not work through the entire catalogue at the pool.

## Summary

**Stage order:** Numbers show the recommended reading and working order, not a compulsory completion sequence. Enter the stage matching the present question while respecting its stated prerequisites. Revisit an earlier stage when new evidence warrants it. A stage number is not a stable identity.

This package supports self-coaching of breaststroke and front crawl. Its Practices contain complete local stage instructions, while Concepts, Principles, Models, Methods and Reference records provide reusable detail. Self-coaching means directing the question, not swimming alone or diagnosing pain.

## Safety and entry boundary

Self-coaching does not mean swimming alone. Use a suitable lifeguarded pool with a buddy and the close assistance needed; weak or inexperienced swimmers need an appropriately capable helper within reach. Attempt only tasks in which breathing, stopping and regaining support are already dependable; otherwise use land study or in-person instruction first. Do not hyperventilate, restrict breaths, prolong submersion or force painful joint positions. Stop for pain, dizziness, distress or unreliable air/support; get immediate assistance for serious symptoms.

## Stage1 Choose a question

**Summary**

**Goal:** Select a small useful task.

**Why:** Attempting to change many features obscures what happened. **What to do:** Name the stroke, observed issue and safe finish. **What to observe:** Whether the issue is felt, visible or measured. **Success signal:** One clear question.

**Next:** Try the stage below, or obtain help if safety is uncertain.

**Procedure**

1. Use a movement reference when you do not know what the action should look like. Use troubleshooting when a specific event is already observed.
2. Write “I feel my hips dip” separately from “a side view shows the hips move downward during the breath.” Do not infer one cause from either statement.
3. Choose a familiar finishable segment or supported rehearsal. No compulsory length or count is needed.

## Stage2 Understand and try one safe adjustment

**Summary**

**Goal:** Test a plausible change.

**Why:** A cue needs a reason and an observation, not blind repetition. **What to do:** Read the relevant stage, predict a signal and try one comfortable adjustment. **What to observe:** Execution, air access and the whole-task effect. **Success signal:** An interpretable result without lost control.

**Next:** Record the result in the next stage; stop for distress or pain.

**Procedure**

1. Read the local stage Summary and procedure. You should know the setup, movement, expected signal and fallback without opening the deeper links.
2. State one alternative explanation and what would count against the selected change. Do not create a deliberately bad or painful control.
3. Keep distance, start, rest and intended effort reasonably comparable. If using a drill, return to ordinary whole-stroke swimming only when that is already safe.
4. Check that the intended movement changed before attributing an outcome to it. An observer or permitted video can locate events but cannot diagnose injury or directly measure hydrodynamic forces.

## Stage3 Record and choose the next action

**Summary**

**Goal:** Update only from actual evidence.

**Why:** A useful local choice is not a universal technique law. **What to do:** Record observation, interpretation and decision separately. **What to observe:** Confounders, contrary signals and transfer. **Success signal:** A justified provisional next step.

**Next:** Revisit the stage or finish for today.

**Procedure**

1. Record what happened in a short note: question / change / observation / uncertainty / next.
2. Choose keep provisionally, modify, discard, unresolved or get help. Rest or end the session when attention, breathing or control deteriorates.
3. Update the stroke’s Current Practice Summary only after actual observation. A template contains no claim about your present technique or distance.

## Local evidence and limits

Coaching sources inform candidate strokes; physics and physiology explain constraints; research scopes the support rather than proving the complete workflow. A safe personal comparison is not a controlled clinical experiment. Ordinary surface pool strokes are the scope; diving, race underwater work, open water and rehabilitation are not taught here.
