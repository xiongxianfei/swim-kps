# Check readiness for todays self coaching task

## Key takeaway

Decide whether the proposed task is safely testable now, or needs in-person instruction first.

## Summary

A technique comparison cannot be useful when loss of air, support or control dominates the task. Directing your own question does not replace supervision. If the selected task requires a floating-to-standing transition I cannot reliably perform, today’s result is “not ready for this unsupported task.” Study the movement on land or obtain in-person instruction instead.

## Inputs and prerequisites

Self-coaching does not mean swimming alone. Use a suitable lifeguarded pool with a buddy and the close assistance needed; weak or inexperienced swimmers need an appropriately capable helper within reach. Attempt only tasks in which breathing, stopping and regaining support are already dependable; otherwise use land study or in-person instruction first. Do not hyperventilate, restrict breaths, prolong submersion or force painful joint positions. Stop for pain, dizziness, distress or unreliable air/support; get immediate assistance for serious symptoms.

## Objective
Decide whether the proposed task is safely testable now, or needs in-person instruction first.

## Rationale
A technique comparison cannot be useful when loss of air, support or control dominates the task. Directing your own question does not replace supervision.

## Evidence and reasoning
[Swimming Safety](../references/american-red-cross-undated-swimming-safety.md#supervision-companions-and-close-assistance) supports lifeguards, a companion and close support for inexperienced swimmers. [Fatal and Nonfatal Drowning Outcomes Related to Dangerous Underwater Breath-Holding Behaviors — New York State, 1988–2011](../references/boyd-2015-dangerous-underwater-breath-holding.md#dangerous-underwater-breathing-behavior) supports excluding dangerous breath-holding behaviours. The checklist is an authored implementation.

## Steps
1. Choose a lifeguarded pool, suitable companion and a task within your current ability to stop and regain support. Follow local pool rules.
2. Check today’s pain, illness, unusual breathlessness, fatigue and confidence. Do not use a technique experiment to diagnose symptoms.
3. Name your support/exit point before entering the task. If immersion, breathing or recovery is not reliable, use qualified in-person instruction; study the knowledge out of water meanwhile.
4. Exclude hyperventilation, breath-hold distance attempts, forced joint positions, solitary drills and deliberate loss of control.
5. Select one question and a short finishable attempt. Stop at the first loss of safe breathing, orientation or control—not at a target count.

## Expected effect
A safe scope, an explicit stopping condition, or a decision not to run the water test.

## Validation and counterevidence
If you cannot state how the attempt ends safely, the gate is not passed. A lifeguard is not a substitute for the close assistance an inexperienced swimmer needs.

## Limits
This is not a water-competence certification. Sudden severe symptoms require prompt help, not another trial.
