# Compare one safe adjustment

## Key takeaway

Test a candidate change against a safe current or reference movement.

## Summary

A planned comparison is more interpretable when the changed feature and expected result are specified beforehand. It remains a personal comparison, not proof of a mechanism. Compare a safe usual arm action with a modest intended compactness change. If order, rest or effort differ, record that uncertainty. Do not deliberately make the baseline painful, exhausting or unsafe.

## Inputs and prerequisites

Self-coaching does not mean swimming alone. Use a suitable lifeguarded pool with a buddy and the close assistance needed; weak or inexperienced swimmers need an appropriately capable helper within reach. Attempt only tasks in which breathing, stopping and regaining support are already dependable; otherwise use land study or in-person instruction first. Do not hyperventilate, restrict breaths, prolong submersion or force painful joint positions. Stop for pain, dizziness, distress or unreliable air/support; get immediate assistance for serious symptoms.

## Local explanatory basis

A sensation, visible motion or single performance change can be consistent with more than one explanation. Performance under a simplified drill’s conditions does not by itself establish performance under whole-stroke conditions.

## Objective
Test a candidate change against a safe current or reference movement.

## Rationale
A planned comparison is more interpretable when the changed feature and expected result are specified beforehand. It remains a personal comparison, not proof of a mechanism.

## Evidence and reasoning
[Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions, version 6.5](../references/cochrane-2024-handbook-v6-5.md#indirectness-relative-to-the-target-question) informs the question of comparability. [Self-coaching hypothesis and decision model](../models/self-coaching-model.md) applies it to individual use with explicit confounders; no efficacy trial of this exact protocol is claimed.

## Steps
1. Pass [Check readiness for today’s self-coaching task](check-readiness.md). Both versions must be comfortable and safe; do not create a deliberately painful, breathless or exaggerated “bad” control.
2. Write the proposed adjustment, expected observation and one outcome that would count against keeping it.
3. Use the same manageable task, similar effort, ordinary rest and comparable start. Record deviations rather than claiming perfect control.
4. If both versions remain safe, repeat on another occasion or vary order to expose order/fatigue effects. Stop when control deteriorates, not when a planned quota is incomplete.
5. Check whether the intended change occurred. Return to whole stroke after any isolated exercise.
6. Choose keep provisionally, modify, discard, unresolved or seek help. Record why and what remains unexplained.

## Expected effect
A better-informed personal choice and an explicit account of uncertainty.

## Validation and counterevidence
No difference, opposite effect, inconsistent execution, worsened breathing or pain counts against adoption. A single good repetition is insufficient to declare a general technique law.

## Limits
No fixed number of attempts establishes causality. Do not increase force, restrict breaths or remove needed support to “control” the experiment.
