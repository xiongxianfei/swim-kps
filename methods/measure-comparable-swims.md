# Measure comparable swims

## Key takeaway

Use simple measurements without reducing technique quality to a single count.

## Summary

Time, count, effort and execution constrain interpretation differently. Measurement conditions can change the apparent result. A lower count with slower time and higher effort is a trade-off, not automatic improvement. Record the exact count convention and any push-off difference before drawing a conclusion.

## Inputs and prerequisites

Self-coaching does not mean swimming alone. Use a suitable lifeguarded pool with a buddy and the close assistance needed; weak or inexperienced swimmers need an appropriately capable helper within reach. Attempt only tasks in which breathing, stopping and regaining support are already dependable; otherwise use land study or in-person instruction first. Do not hyperventilate, restrict breaths, prolong submersion or force painful joint positions. Stop for pain, dizziness, distress or unreliable air/support; get immediate assistance for serious symptoms.

## Local explanatory basis

Stroke count alone does not determine speed, metabolic cost or whole-stroke effectiveness.

## Objective
Use simple measurements without reducing technique quality to a single count.

## Rationale
Time, count, effort and execution constrain interpretation differently. Measurement conditions can change the apparent result.

## Evidence and reasoning
[Comparable-swim measurement model](../models/stroke-measurement-model.md) is a definitional and practical model. [Fewer strokes do not identify lower energy cost](../principles/fewer-strokes-do-not-identify-lower-energy-cost.md) states the non-identifiability explicitly.

## Steps
1. Choose a familiar safe distance or event segment; do not add distance for measurement convenience.
2. Define one breaststroke cycle or a front-crawl arm entry/two-arm cycle before counting.
3. Record distance, start/stop, push-off, rest and assistance. Have a companion time when that avoids distraction.
4. Record comfortable effort and breathing alongside time/count. Mark inaccurate or missing observations instead of filling them.
5. Compare like tasks and inspect trade-offs. Do not call lower count, longer glide or faster time alone “efficiency.”

## Expected effect
A small set of observations with known conventions and limitations.

## Validation and counterevidence
Variable push-off, effort, distance or count definition can invalidate the comparison. More decimal places do not fix those differences.

## Limits
No maximum-effort testing, oxygen-consumption estimate or injury-risk inference from these measures.
