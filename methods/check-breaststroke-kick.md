# Check breaststroke kick preparation and completion

## Key takeaway

Observe whether foot preparation, kick and completion are happening comfortably before adding effort.

## Summary

More effort need not improve an interaction whose orientation or timing is not yet usable. Slowing preparation may reveal an omission, but it is only one diagnostic aid. Question: do the feet prepare before the main action? First inspect that event in a comfortable supported setup. If moving to a free swim, keep air access and recovery reliable and check whether preparation remains visible.

## Inputs and prerequisites

Self-coaching does not mean swimming alone. Use a suitable lifeguarded pool with a buddy and the close assistance needed; weak or inexperienced swimmers need an appropriately capable helper within reach. Attempt only tasks in which breathing, stopping and regaining support are already dependable; otherwise use land study or in-person instruction first. Do not hyperventilate, restrict breaths, prolong submersion or force painful joint positions. Stop for pain, dizziness, distress or unreliable air/support; get immediate assistance for serious symptoms.

## Local explanatory basis

Changing limb orientation and motion can change the direction and useful contribution of the interaction with water. Performance under a simplified drill’s conditions does not by itself establish performance under whole-stroke conditions. A pain report at a joint does not uniquely identify the movement error or tissue condition responsible.

## Objective
Observe whether foot preparation, kick and completion are happening comfortably before adding effort.

## Rationale
More effort need not improve an interaction whose orientation or timing is not yet usable. Slowing preparation may reveal an omission, but it is only one diagnostic aid.

## Evidence and reasoning
[Improving your kicking in breaststroke](../references/swim-england-2018-improving-breaststroke-kick.md#foot-preparation-and-kick-completion) and [Breaststroke Kick: The Complete Guide](../references/us-masters-swimming-undated-breaststroke-kick.md#preparation-kick-direction-and-individual-width) describe preparation/completion and individual variation. They do not diagnose pain or establish a compulsory pause.

## Steps
1. Choose dry rehearsal or secure wall-supported exploration with a known air/recovery route. No forced feet, knees, maximal kick or vertical-kicking task.
2. Rehearse heel recovery, gentle toe-to-shin flexion and comfortable outward foot preparation. The hip, knee and ankle participate; do not mechanically wrench the feet outward.
3. In the supported movement, bring heels toward the seat without deliberately hauling the knees toward the chest. Do not use identical shoulder-width knees as a pass criterion.
4. If preparation is visibly skipped, slow only that event enough to notice it. A checking pause is a temporary aid, not a mandatory one-second stage.
5. Move the prepared feet with a backward component and complete toward long legs. Rounded/direct descriptions differ between sources; do not force an exact geometric path.
6. At a wall, observe sequence and comfort rather than travel. If a free short stroke is already safe, check preparation, forward movement and breathing together.
7. If the movement deteriorates when air is needed, return to support, stop, recover and breathe before another attempt. Do not expect the kick alone to lift the mouth.
8. For pain, stop the task and seek appropriate assessment. For a promising change, test whole-stroke transfer at manageable demand.

## Expected effect
More observable preparation and completion without forced range or increased discomfort.

## Validation and counterevidence
If preparation changes but movement or coordination does not improve, revise the hypothesis. Isolated success does not establish full-stroke benefit.

## Limits
No maximal kick, vertical-kicking exercise, prescribed joint angle or claim that turnout cures knee pain.

## Setup alternatives and non equivalence
Dry or stable supported rehearsal is for sequence, not force measurement. A secure wall hold removes travel; a kickboard changes support and breathing. Neither represents a full stroke. A supported kick–stop–recover–breathe sequence can reconnect attention to air without claiming that the kick should lift the mouth. Use an ordinary short stroke for transfer only when safe. The Practice contains a context-specific version of this bridge.

The source families differ in their rounded versus direct-backward wording. No exact width/path or maximal turnout is resolved by their agreement about preparation. Candidate movement and individual comfort must be checked separately.
