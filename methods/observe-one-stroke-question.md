# Observe one stroke question

## Key takeaway

Turn a vague difficulty into a describable event without assuming its cause.

## Summary

“I feel my legs sink” and “my legs visibly angle lower after the breath” are different observations. Neither alone identifies the mechanism. Write “I felt hurried at the breath” before writing “my head rises too far.” Ask for a permitted side observation of one event or record that the necessary view is unavailable.

## Inputs and prerequisites

Self-coaching does not mean swimming alone. Use a suitable lifeguarded pool with a buddy and the close assistance needed; weak or inexperienced swimmers need an appropriately capable helper within reach. Attempt only tasks in which breathing, stopping and regaining support are already dependable; otherwise use land study or in-person instruction first. Do not hyperventilate, restrict breaths, prolong submersion or force painful joint positions. Stop for pain, dizziness, distress or unreliable air/support; get immediate assistance for serious symptoms.

## Local explanatory basis

A sensation, visible motion or single performance change can be consistent with more than one explanation.

## Objective
Turn a vague difficulty into a describable event without assuming its cause.

## Rationale
“I feel my legs sink” and “my legs visibly angle lower after the breath” are different observations. Neither alone identifies the mechanism.

## Evidence and reasoning
[Observation does not identify one cause](../principles/observation-does-not-identify-one-cause.md) and [Self-coaching hypothesis and decision model](../models/self-coaching-model.md) motivate the separation. This is a record-making method, not an independently validated diagnostic test.

## Steps
1. Choose one event already within safe ability: for example the breath and the next arm recovery.
2. Write what you feel, what an observer sees and what was measured in separate sentences. Mark unavailable evidence as unavailable.
3. Note when the issue occurs relative to the stroke, pace and fatigue. Avoid global labels such as “bad technique.”
4. Write one plausible explanation and one alternative, or “not enough observation.”
5. Choose the smallest useful next check: suitable view, supported rehearsal, easier whole-stroke attempt or outside feedback.

## Expected effect
A focused question with observable features and unresolved alternatives.

## Validation and counterevidence
An observation can be repeated or shown to another person without embedding the favored cause. If not, rewrite it.

## Limits
Do not generate multiple unsafe tests to distinguish explanations. Sensation is valid as a report, not as a direct force measurement.
