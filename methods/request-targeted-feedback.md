# Request targeted outside feedback

## Key takeaway

Obtain the kind of observation or assessment that a personal record cannot supply.

## Summary

External observation can resolve a visible movement question; symptoms may require a different kind of professional assessment. Neither is replaced by stronger confidence in a cue. Ask an instructor what they observe at one stroke event. For persistent or worsening pain, seek healthcare assessment instead of requesting a biomechanical diagnosis from the video.

## Inputs and prerequisites

A concrete question, the material or actual record to inspect, and enough context to distinguish observed information from a proposed interpretation. No result is assumed in advance.

## Local explanatory basis

A pain report at a joint does not uniquely identify the movement error or tissue condition responsible.

## Objective
Obtain the kind of observation or assessment that a personal record cannot supply.

## Rationale
External observation can resolve a visible movement question; symptoms may require a different kind of professional assessment. Neither is replaced by stronger confidence in a cue.

## Evidence and reasoning
[Swimming Safety](../references/american-red-cross-undated-swimming-safety.md#supervision-companions-and-close-assistance) addresses support for inexperienced swimmers. [Knee pain](../references/nhs-undated-knee-pain.md#assessment-boundaries-for-knee-symptoms) and [Shoulder pain](../references/nhs-undated-shoulder-pain.md#assessment-boundaries-for-shoulder-symptoms) address symptom escalation rather than stroke diagnosis.

## Steps
1. For a technique question, ask a qualified swimming instructor to observe one event and explain what they actually see before accepting a cause.
2. Share the target task, hypothesis, safe comparison and result—not just the label “bad technique.”
3. For pain, stop the aggravating activity and seek appropriate healthcare assessment, particularly for persistent/worsening symptoms or problems with ordinary movement.
4. Use urgent help for severe or sudden symptoms, major swelling/deformity, inability to move/use the limb, fever with a hot/swollen joint, or significant breathing distress as appropriate.
5. Record the professional’s scope and actual advice. A coach’s observation is not a medical diagnosis; a clinical finding is not a universal swimming principle.

## Expected effect
A better observation, qualified plan or appropriate referral.

## Validation and counterevidence
The requested expertise matches the question. If advice is unclear or aggravates symptoms, clarify rather than force execution.

## Limits
This package does not provide rehabilitation, diagnosis or clearance to return after injury. Emergencies require local emergency services.
