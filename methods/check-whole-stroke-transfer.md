# Check whole stroke transfer

## Key takeaway

Determine whether a simplified exercise contributes to the intended swimming task.

## Summary

Simplification changes task demands. Its usefulness depends on what returns to ordinary swimming, not how well the simplified form looks. After an arm rehearsal, return to one familiar short swim with normal breathing. Check whether the same arm action appears and whether comfort or coordination improves, stays unchanged or worsens.

## Inputs and prerequisites

Self-coaching does not mean swimming alone. Use a suitable lifeguarded pool with a buddy and the close assistance needed; weak or inexperienced swimmers need an appropriately capable helper within reach. Attempt only tasks in which breathing, stopping and regaining support are already dependable; otherwise use land study or in-person instruction first. Do not hyperventilate, restrict breaths, prolong submersion or force painful joint positions. Stop for pain, dizziness, distress or unreliable air/support; get immediate assistance for serious symptoms.

## Local explanatory basis

Performance under a simplified drill’s conditions does not by itself establish performance under whole-stroke conditions.

## Objective
Determine whether a simplified exercise contributes to the intended swimming task.

## Rationale
Simplification changes task demands. Its usefulness depends on what returns to ordinary swimming, not how well the simplified form looks.

## Evidence and reasoning
[Isolated success does not establish transfer](../principles/isolated-success-does-not-establish-transfer.md) gives the reasoning. This protocol is an authored comparison, not a claim that isolated practice generally outperforms whole practice.

## Steps
1. State what the target whole stroke should show and what the simpler exercise removed: support, arm action, breathing or timing demands.
2. Select the easier exercise only if it is safe and addresses that question. Keep ordinary air access and a clear end.
3. Return to a familiar short whole stroke; do not remove necessary safety support to claim transfer.
4. Record whether the intended movement occurred and whether breathing, coordination and comfort survived. Both are needed for a useful personal decision.
5. If the drill succeeds but the stroke fails, inspect task demand and the first event mismatch. Simplify the cue, reduce demand or request help.
6. Recheck later without the drill/cue when safe. Retention and transfer are not established by one successful repetition.

## Expected effect
A reason to retain or discard a specific drill in a specific Practice.

## Validation and counterevidence
Excellent drill performance without target-task improvement does not meet the purpose. A failed whole-stroke attempt may instead signal excess demand or missing readiness.

## Limits
Do not remove essential support just to claim transfer. Some skills need direct in-person teaching.
