# Record permitted video for a specific question

## Key takeaway

Obtain a relevant external view of a visible movement while protecting safety and privacy.

## Summary

Video may distinguish event order or gross movement that is difficult to feel, but it cannot directly establish force, pain causes or physiological cost. Ask a companion to film the chosen event from a permitted safe position with consent. A side clip can clarify timing; it cannot measure hidden three-dimensional forces or diagnose a painful structure.

## Inputs and prerequisites

Self-coaching does not mean swimming alone. Use a suitable lifeguarded pool with a buddy and the close assistance needed; weak or inexperienced swimmers need an appropriately capable helper within reach. Attempt only tasks in which breathing, stopping and regaining support are already dependable; otherwise use land study or in-person instruction first. Do not hyperventilate, restrict breaths, prolong submersion or force painful joint positions. Stop for pain, dizziness, distress or unreliable air/support; get immediate assistance for serious symptoms.

## Evidence and reasoning
[Self-coaching hypothesis and decision model](../models/self-coaching-model.md) and [Comparable-swim measurement model](../models/stroke-measurement-model.md) limit interpretation. The filming plan is authored; it is not a clinical movement screen.

## Steps
1. Obtain pool permission and consent from anyone identifiable. Avoid changing-room filming and capturing bystanders; follow local rules.
2. Ask a companion to record from an allowed position while you swim normally. Do not hold a phone or manage filming during the stroke.
3. Choose the view needed: side for timing/body motion, an appropriate front view for entry/asymmetry. Do not infer a hidden plane from one angle.
4. Label the clip with stroke, task and date. Describe a small number of visible events before explaining them.
5. Compare the intended change with the actual movement. Keep recordings privately and delete unnecessary footage.

## Expected effect
A bounded visual observation that can support or challenge a movement hypothesis.

## Validation and counterevidence
If the relevant limb/event is obscured, do not score it. Camera perspective and water refraction limit apparent angles.

## Limits
No unsafe underwater setup, privacy intrusion or exact 3D measurement from an ordinary clip. External observation may be more useful than another camera.
