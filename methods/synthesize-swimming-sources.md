# Synthesize sources for a swimming question

## Key takeaway

Find and compare the evidence actually needed for a movement question without treating a discipline label as proof.

## Summary

A physics relationship, a coaching cue and a trained-swimmer experiment have different evidentiary roles. Several pages can repeat one source family. For an arm correction, distinguish the physical relation, measured stroke behavior, coaching recommendation and expected personal benefit. Several coaching pages can agree without supplying an effectiveness trial.

## Inputs and prerequisites

A concrete question, the material or actual record to inspect, and enough context to distinguish observed information from a proposed interpretation. No result is assumed in advance.

## Local explanatory basis

A sensation, visible motion or single performance change can be consistent with more than one explanation.

## Objective
Find and compare the evidence actually needed for a movement question without treating a discipline label as proof.

## Rationale
A physics relationship, a coaching cue and a trained-swimmer experiment have different evidentiary roles. Several pages can repeat one source family.

## Evidence and reasoning
[Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions, version 6.5](../references/cochrane-2024-handbook-v6-5.md#reports-and-underlying-studies) distinguishes reports from studies; [Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions, version 6.5](../references/cochrane-2024-handbook-v6-5.md#indirectness-relative-to-the-target-question) addresses applicability. Their adaptation here is a practical research procedure, not a systematic review.

## Steps
1. State the precise question and goal: for example whether a specific arm correction is a reasonable reference for comfortable surface breaststroke.
2. Split the claim into physical relation, actual stroke behavior, instructional choice and expected individual effect.
3. Find source material relevant to each uncertain part. Prefer direct accessible content; record abstract-only access, missing dates and unresolved corrections.
4. Create one publication-identity reference record. Record exact claim locations, independence/common upstream work and what it does not establish.
5. Compare support and challenges. Two agreeing coaching families are guidance convergence, not two efficacy experiments.
6. Write source-supported premises separately from the inferred choice and its limits. Revise the claim when evidence is weak; do not keep searching only for support.
7. Link the synthesis to a Method’s rationale and define a safe observation that could count against the choice.

## Expected effect
A claim-specific evidence account, a qualified candidate action, or a documented unresolved question.

## Validation and counterevidence
Every important assertion has an inspected support location or an explicit inference/convention label. A single authoritative source may suffice for its own definition; a source quota is not the criterion.

## Limits
No clinical diagnosis from research snippets, paid-text claim without access, or universal rule derived from elite performance. External sources do not confirm your current technique.

## Worked synthesis

## Local evidence summary

[Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions, version 6.5](../references/cochrane-2024-handbook-v6-5.md#indirectness-relative-to-the-target-question) contributes: Evidence may be indirect when study populations, interventions, comparisons or outcomes differ from the target question.

Citation identifies support for the stated claim and scope. Any further causal or practical inference remains separately reviewable.
