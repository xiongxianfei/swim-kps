# Body alignment

## Key takeaway

Alignment describes the relation of body segments; it is not a rigid pose to force.

## Summary

Body alignment describes where head, torso, pelvis and limbs are relative to one another and to the direction of travel. It changes through breathing, recovery and propulsion. A useful body line permits the chosen task, breathing and comfortable movement rather than simply matching a still image.

## Definition and use

The relative arrangement of head, trunk and limbs for the current stroke task.

Observe when the body changes: during the breath, while heels recover, or as a hand enters. “My hips feel lower” is a report; a side view may help locate the event. A brief supported shape and active swimming are different tasks.

## Boundary and non example

No exact gaze angle, permanent horizontal-leg requirement or locked-core cue follows from this definition. Pain or loss of air access is not a reason to force the picture.
